# Enterprise LMS procurement gate: what 1,000+ employee buyers require before signing

Scope: the IT/security/legal/procurement gate for LMS vendors, not learning features. Findings are drawn from vendor-RFP checklists, security-questionnaire guides, DPA/data-residency guides, accessibility procurement guides, and SLA/API benchmarks published 2024–2026. Confidence varies — most are practitioner blog consensus rather than peer-reviewed survey data, so treat percentages as directional, not precise.

## Ranked: how often each item is a dealbreaker

| Rank | Requirement | Typical status at 1,000+ FTE | Typical status at <500 FTE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SSO (SAML 2.0 or OIDC), IdP-agnostic (Azure AD/Entra ID, Okta) | Hard requirement, "table stakes not a premium upsell" | Often nice-to-have or paid add-on |
| 2 | SOC 2 Type II report (or ISO 27001 cert) | Hard gate — deal typically cannot get past InfoSec without it | Nice-to-have, sometimes just self-attestation |
| 3 | Security questionnaire completion (SIG/CAIQ) + InfoSec sign-off | Hard gate, formal step in the buying process | Rare; usually skipped |
| 4 | MFA support/enforcement | Hard requirement, tested directly in vendor security reviews | Expected but rarely blocking |
| 5 | SCIM 2.0 provisioning + automated de-provisioning | Hard requirement for larger IT orgs; treated as a breach-risk control | Nice-to-have; manual CSV/admin provisioning tolerated |
| 6 | DPA / GDPR data-processing terms | Hard requirement wherever EU personal data is processed | Hard requirement only if EU users exist; otherwise skipped |
| 7 | Audit logs (immutable, exportable) | Hard requirement, often explicitly scored in RFPs | Nice-to-have |
| 8 | Recent penetration-test report (<12 months, third-party) | Hard requirement in security review; a stale/missing report stalls or kills the deal | Rarely requested |
| 9 | Data residency commitments (region-pinned storage) | Hard requirement for EU, healthcare, government, financial-services buyers; negotiable elsewhere | Rarely requested |
| 10 | Uptime SLA (99.9%+, credits, status page) | Expected in contract; negotiable terms but presence is close to mandatory at six-figure deal sizes | Nice-to-have |
| 11 | WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA conformance + VPAT/ACR | Hard requirement for government, education, and increasingly regulated/EU private-sector buyers; elsewhere still "nice-to-have but rising" | Rarely requested outside public sector |
| 12 | EN 301 549 / European Accessibility Act (EAA) conformance | Hard legal requirement for any vendor selling to EU public bodies or serving EU consumers since the June 2025 EAA deadline | Not yet relevant |
| 13 | REST API + webhooks | Expected, scored as a checklist item; blocking only when deep HRIS/BI integration is required | Nice-to-have |
| 14 | Native HRIS sync (Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, BambooHR) | Nice-to-have for large enterprises — SCIM/API middleware is an accepted substitute; native connector saves time but rarely a hard blocker | Not requested |
| 15 | Localisation / multi-language UI | Nice-to-have unless the buyer has non-English-speaking workforce segments (then hard requirement for those locales only) | Rarely requested |

## Detail by item

**SSO (SAML 2.0, OIDC, Azure AD/Entra ID, Okta).** Enterprise RFP security baselines list SSO for IdPs like Okta/Auth0/Entra as a non-negotiable line item alongside SCIM and RBAC — [SSO & Security in RFP Software: 2026 Buyer's Guide](https://easyhotelrfp.com/blog/sso-security-rfp-software/). Enterprise LMS technical checklists likewise put SAML 2.0/OIDC support ahead of any content-related spec — [Enterprise LMS Technical Specifications Checklist (2026)](https://blog.braincert.com/the-enterprise-lms-technical-specifications-checklist-12-specs-buyers-actually-need-to-verify/).

**SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001.** A current SOC 2 or ISO 27001 report "answers, or removes the need for, large parts of" a vendor security questionnaire — [SIG vs CAIQ vs VSAQ (2026)](https://www.diligio.co/guides/security-questionnaire-types). High-impact suppliers undergo annual review of SOC 2 reports and ISO 27001 certificates as standard practice — [VRM Security Questionnaires: SIG, CAIQ & CIS Controls](https://www.bitsight.com/blog/vrm-security-questionnaires-sig-caiq-cis-controls).

**Security questionnaires (SIG/CAIQ).** SIG (Standardized Information Gathering) evaluates 18 risk-control domains and covers enterprise risk, privacy and operational resilience broadly; CAIQ is CSA's 259-question cloud-specific questionnaire — [CAIQ vs SIG](https://www.hypercomply.com/blog/caiq-vs-sig), [SIG Security Questionnaire Explained](https://akitra.com/blog/sig-security-questionnaires/). Both map directly onto ISO 27001 controls, so responses double as audit evidence — [Bitsight, ibid.](https://www.bitsight.com/blog/vrm-security-questionnaires-sig-caiq-cis-controls)

**MFA and SCIM/de-provisioning.** Vendor security reviews explicitly assess SSO/MFA capability and expect MFA enforced across all accounts — [Vendor Security Assessment: The Enterprise Checklist](https://www.iteratorshq.com/blog/vendor-security-assessment-the-enterprise-checklist-saas-startups-miss/). Automated SCIM de-provisioning is treated as a direct control against ex-employee access risk, citing that roughly 1 in 5 orgs has suffered a breach tied to a former employee's still-active account — [Enterprise LMS Technical Specifications Checklist](https://blog.braincert.com/the-enterprise-lms-technical-specifications-checklist-12-specs-buyers-actually-need-to-verify/).

**Pen test reports.** Standard cadence is annual third-party testing; a report older than 12 months "raises more questions than having no report at all," and a ready report can turn a two-week security review into two days — [Vendor Security Assessment Penetration Test Guide 2026](https://www.pentesttesting.com/vendor-security-assessment-penetration-test/).

**GDPR/DPA, data residency, retention & deletion, audit logs.** GDPR requires a written DPA between controller and processor covering data types, purposes, retention, subprocessors and breach notice — [The SaaS DPA Guide](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/data-processing-agreements-dpas-for-saas), [Data Processing Agreement: SaaS Guide](https://www.reform.app/blog/data-processing-agreement-saas-guide). Enterprise/EU customers increasingly require per-tenant regional data routing and DPA language on cross-border transfer mechanisms — [Data Residency for SaaS](https://www.alation.com/blog/data-residency-by-design-global-compliance/). Neither GDPR nor CCPA fix retention periods, but vendors must define and evidence their own, with deletion needing proof it reached backups — [Data Retention Policy for SaaS (2026)](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/data-retention-policy-saas-startup-guide-2026), [DPDPA Compliance Checklist](https://www.archondatastore.com/blog/dpdpa-compliance-checklist/). Vendor due-diligence checklists list audit-log capability as a standard line item alongside SOC 2/ISO certification — [SaaS Vendor Due Diligence Checklist](https://www.rfp.wiki/content/saas-vendor-due-diligence-security-compliance-checklist).

**Accessibility: WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA, VPAT/ACR, EN 301 549, EAA.** WCAG 2.1 AA is the practical benchmark procurement teams check for; language is starting to shift toward 2.2 — [What Procurement Teams Check for in an ACR](https://accessible.org/procurement-teams-check-acr/). VPATs come in WCAG, Section 508, EU (EN 301 549) and INT editions, and the completed VPAT (the ACR) is what procurement actually reviews before purchase — [What is a VPAT?](https://www.nutrient.io/blog/what-is-a-vpat/), [VPAT/EAA Compliance Explained](https://www.viewsonic.com/library/business/what-is-vpat-eaa-compliance-explained/). The European Accessibility Act imposes binding EN 301 549-aligned obligations on vendors selling digital services (including e-learning) into the EU, with enforcement live from June 2025 — [European Accessibility Act Compliance Guide 2026](https://getwcag.com/en/what-is-european-accessibility-act-eaa). Outside government/education/EU-regulated buyers, accessibility is still commonly scored as "nice-to-have" but is rising fast.

**Uptime SLA.** 99.9% is the typical enterprise LMS SLA baseline (99.95% considered enterprise-grade); a public, quantifiable SLA with credits is described as necessary or "six-figure deals will stall in legal review" — [Enterprise SaaS SLA Benchmark](https://vendorbenchmark.com/guides/enterprise-saas-sla-benchmark), [Dedicated Enterprise SLAs & Support Page (2026)](https://truto.one/blog/how-to-create-a-dedicated-enterprise-slas-support-page-2026/).

**REST API, webhooks, HRIS sync (Workday/SuccessFactors/BambooHR).** Enterprise LMS checklists expect REST API + webhooks + a sandbox environment as standard — [Enterprise LMS Technical Specifications Checklist](https://blog.braincert.com/the-enterprise-lms-technical-specifications-checklist-12-specs-buyers-actually-need-to-verify/), [LMS APIs overview](https://360learning.com/blog/lms-apis/). Native Workday/SuccessFactors connectors are common among enterprise LMS vendors and valued for auto-provisioning new hires, but SCIM/API-based integration is an accepted substitute, so this functions more as a differentiator than a hard gate — [Enterprise LMS procurement 2026 overview](https://lpi.academy/knowledge/what_is_enterprise_learning_management_software_procurement_and_how_should_ld_teams_evaluate_it_in_2026.php).

**Enterprise vs. midmarket spend/complexity.** Midmarket buyers tolerate looser requirements (manager dashboards, basic data exchange); enterprise buyers reaching six-figure annual spend are the segment that imposes multi-portal, strict-security-review, and global-administration requirements — [How LMS Pricing Models Work (2026)](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/09/24/how-lms-pricing-models-work-and-what-you-should-expect-to-pay/).

## Caveat
Precise percentage stats circulating in vendor-marketing blogs (e.g., "68% of RFPs require X," "78% require SOC 2") could not be traced to a named primary survey and should be treated as illustrative industry framing rather than verified data points.

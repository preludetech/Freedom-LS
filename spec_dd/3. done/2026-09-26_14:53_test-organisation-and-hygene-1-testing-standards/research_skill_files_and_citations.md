### Recommendation: does placement guidance get its own subsection, or fold into "Creating Test Data"?

Recommend **its own named subsection**, not folding into "Creating Test Data," for three reasons
visible in the live structure:

1. "Creating Test Data" (resources/testing.md lines 9-17) is scoped to factories specifically — every
   existing bullet is about how to call/override a factory. The new placement material (root vs.
   app-level conftest, fixture scope, conftest-vs-plain-module) is about fixtures and conftest
   organisation, a materially different question from "how do I get a row into the DB for this test."
   Folding it in would make one section answer two unrelated questions.
2. The file's existing pattern for a self-contained rule-with-worked-example is a same-level `##`
   section with its own subsections when there's enough material to need them (see "Collection safety
   for optional apps" → "The guard (braces)" / "The colocated conftest (belt)", and "Test order
   independence" → "`transaction=True` is expensive"). The placement + scope + thin-wrapper +
   stub-model material this spec adds is comparable in size to "Collection safety for optional apps"
   (a full rule, a worked example, an explanation of what doesn't work), so it fits the file's
   existing granularity as its own section, not a handful of bullets bolted onto an existing one.
3. `SKILL.md` cross-links to `resources/testing.md` by named section today (e.g. "Testing HTMX views"
   → "HTMX test patterns section"). A named "Conftest and fixture placement" (or similar) section
   gives `SKILL.md`'s "Key rules" a precise anchor to link to, the same way it already links to
   "Collection safety for optional apps."

Suggested section name to slot in after "Creating Test Data" and before "Test Patterns": something
like `## conftest, fixtures and factories` (covering conftest-vs-plain-module, fixture placement,
fixture scope/idempotency, the tightened thin-wrapper rule, factory cross-app direction, and the
stub-model technique as subsections) — keeping "Creating Test Data" focused purely on factory usage
as it is today.

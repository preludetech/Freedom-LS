# Research: White-Label / "Powered By" Precedents for Cohort Report Branding

Scope: product/competitive/UX research to inform the FLS feature where cohort progress-report PDFs
should carry the **Organisation's** brand primary, with the platform reduced to a "Powered by
&lt;platform&gt;" mark. This is desk research based on public documentation, help centers, community
forums, and vendor marketing pages — it has **not** been verified against the actual current UI of
each product (no logins were used), so specifics should be treated as "as publicly documented" rather
than "confirmed by hands-on testing."

**General pattern across the table:** every commercial hosted LMS/course platform researched that supports multi-client or multi-brand delivery (Docebo, TalentLMS, Thinkific, Teachable) uses the same shape: a **default "Powered by X" mark that is visible unless explicitly removed**, and removal is gated either by (a) plan tier, or (b) an explicit admin toggle in a "white label" settings area. None of the sources found documented the mark being **impossible** to remove, nor being **on by default with no toggle at all** — some toggle/tier gate was universal where the feature existed at all. Whether that removal extends specifically to **exported PDF artifacts** (vs. just the live web UI) was **not confirmed** for any vendor from public docs alone — flagging this as a real gap worth testing/asking about, and worth being explicit about in FLS's own spec (does "powered by" show on-screen only, or in the generated PDF too — it should probably be both, per the FLS ask).

## 2. The "Powered by" convention itself

- **Tier-gated removal is the dominant pattern in B2B2C SaaS**, not just LMS. Docebo, TalentLMS, Thinkific, and Teachable all follow "on by default, removable at a higher tier or via explicit config." This generalizes beyond LMS: embedded-analytics vendors follow the identical shape — e.g. attribution badges "Powered by Metabase", "Powered by Luzmo" are similarly tier-gated, with full white-labeling reserved for premium/enterprise tiers. [White Label Analytics: A Buyer's Guide](https://www.sigmacomputing.com/blog/white-label-analytics)
- A recurring critique in the SaaS-embedding space: **many products calling themselves "white-label" only let the customer swap the header logo while leaving a "Powered by" badge or the vendor's domain in the footer** — commentary treats this as a "logo swap, not real white-label," implying purchasers do care about the distinction and consider forced attribution a downgrade from a "true" white-label promise. [White Label Analytics: A Buyer's Guide](https://www.sigmacomputing.com/blog/white-label-analytics)
- **Perception is genuinely mixed, and this is relevant to the FLS design decision:** on one hand, "Powered by" marks are sometimes leveraged deliberately as a *trust/quality* signal — e.g., some TalentLMS resellers choose to **keep** "Powered by TalentLMS" on their rebranded product even though removal is available to them, treating it as a positive credibility marker rather than something to hide. [eLearning Industry: 50 Shades of White with TalentLMS](https://elearningindustry.com/white-labeling-with-talentlms). On the other hand, the broader "logo swap, not white-label" critique above suggests forced/non-removable attribution is perceived negatively by buyers who expect full control. Could not find controlled research (survey/A-B test) isolating recipient trust perception of a "powered by" mark specifically on a *downloaded PDF record* (as opposed to a live web app) — this appears to be a genuine research gap, not just something under-documented; treat any claim about "increases/decreases trust on a PDF" as unverified.
- No authoritative, vendor-neutral standard or convention (e.g., an accessibility/trust body position) was found stating "powered by" marks are required or forbidden on exported documents — this is purely a vendor commercial/UX choice space, not a compliance requirement outside specific regulated contexts (see Q6).

## 3. Known UX complaints / support-ticket patterns for branded PDF exports in LMSes

Direct "LMS PDF export branding bug" reports were sparse in public search results (most vendors don't publish their bug trackers), but the **adjacent evidence is strong and directly applicable**, since PDF rendering issues are largely engine-level, not product-level:

- **Blurry/pixelated/stretched logos in PDF exports** are a very well-documented, recurring complaint across PDF-generating tools generally (design tools, but the root causes generalize to any PDF-rendering pipeline): raster logos scaled up beyond their native resolution lose sharpness (e.g., scaling a 300ppi image to 200% halves effective resolution to 150ppi); the standard mitigation is requiring **vector logos (SVG)** or requiring a minimum raster resolution (one source recommends 800ppi at final display size for raster fallbacks). Moodle's `mod_customcert` community threads specifically flag that **placing a logo inside a background/border element results in blurry output**, and that large/fixed-placement logo elements can cause **other certificate text to be pushed off-canvas**. [Adobe Community: logo exporting blurry/pixelated](https://community.adobe.com/questions-652/logo-exporting-blurry-pixelated-how-to-solve-784247), [Moodle forum: Custom Certificate Design](https://moodle.org/mod/forum/discuss.php?d=337273)
- **Logo present on screen but missing/broken in the generated PDF** is a distinctly common class of bug in HTML-to-PDF rendering pipelines (e.g., wkhtmltopdf-style engines): a logo can render correctly in the live web view but fail to load in the PDF version because the PDF renderer resolves image URLs/paths differently (e.g., cannot reach a relative path, an authenticated URL, or a URL that requires cookies/session context that the PDF-rendering process doesn't have). One documented example flagged this exact web-vs-PDF discrepancy as tied to how the PDF engine handles image references. [White Label Reporting for SaaS: The Practical Guide](https://www.toucantoco.com/en/blog/white-label-reporting-for-saas), [Odoo forum: PDF report not showing/loading logo](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/odoo-report-pdf-version-not-showing-loading-image-logo-122518) — **this is directly relevant to FLS's likely PDF-generation approach and worth an explicit test case: "logo displays correctly in the org branding preview, but confirm it also renders in the actual generated PDF, including for organisation logos stored via URL/file field rather than inline asset."**
- **Transparent-background logos rendering with an unwanted colored background/box in PDF** is a widely reported class of complaint across PDF tools (Adobe Acrobat, InDesign, OpenOffice, print pipelines) — a PNG logo with a transparent background can pick up a white or otherwise incorrect background box when composited into a PDF, especially if the rendering pipeline flattens transparency incorrectly or the logo sits on a colored report header/band. This is directly relevant if FLS org logos are uploaded as arbitrary PNGs and placed on a colored report header — **the org's brand color band + a logo with transparency is exactly the combination known to cause visible artifacts** (e.g., a faint white halo/box around the logo). [Adobe Community: transparent logo occurring when PDF printed](https://community.adobe.com/t5/indesign-discussions/how-do-i-eliminate-coloured-background-on-transparent-logo-occurring-when-pdf-printed/m-p/9342781), [Reach Reporting: transparency/color changes when printing a PDF](https://knowledge.reachreporting.com/common-issue-transparency-or-color-changes-when-printing-a-pdf)
- **Wrong tenant's logo/branding appearing** is a recognized multi-tenant SaaS failure mode, generally rooted in missing tenant-context propagation: e.g., background/async jobs (report generation is very often an async job) running **without proper tenant context**, un-namespaced cache keys, or a missing tenant filter on the query that fetches "the" org logo. General multi-tenant SaaS engineering guidance explicitly calls out background jobs as one of the "overlooked layers" where cross-tenant bugs originate — **directly relevant since PDF report generation for FLS is likely to run as a background/async task**, and the org-logo lookup inside that task must be scoped correctly (and re-verified, not assumed inherited from request context). [Multi-Tenant SaaS Mistakes I'll Never Make Again (Medium)](https://medium.com/@Fahad06/multi-tenant-saas-mistakes-ill-never-make-again-ef358e1feb9f), [Bugstrix: Multi-Tenant SaaS Security Testing](https://bugstrix.com/blogs/multi-tenant-saas-security-testing-how-to-prevent-cross-tenant-data-leaks/)
- **Org name too long for the layout / text overflow** is a generic, well-documented UI/document-generation problem class: text can overflow its container when a report/certificate layout was designed assuming a shortish name, and generic mitigations are (a) a hard length cap with validation at data-entry time, (b) dynamic/auto-shrinking font sizing, or (c) truncation with an accessible full-value fallback (e.g., a tooltip, in a print-PDF context this would instead be something like ensuring the full name is still present in text/metadata even if visually wrapped). One direct precedent: an Open edX / general certificate-generation discussion recommends templates whose name field **dynamically adjusts width/font-size** rather than fixing it, specifically to avoid needing multiple hand-maintained layout variants for different name lengths. Some domains (e.g., X.509 certificate `OrganizationName`) simply hard-cap the field (64 chars) at data-entry time rather than solving it at render time — a simpler, cheaper mitigation worth considering for FLS. [Microsoft Q&A: adjust font size for different length certificate names](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4812668/best-way-s-to-automatically-adjust-font-size-for-d), [D2L: troubleshooting certificate text too long](https://boris.sdbor.edu/d2ldocs/InstructorHelp_201912/le/awards/instructor/troubleshooting_long_text_on_certificate.htm)
- **Not explicitly found but worth flagging as a plausible risk pattern by analogy**: "branding applied to the web UI but not to reports" — no direct forum thread was found describing this exact complaint for an LMS, but it is the natural consequence of the same root cause as the "logo missing in PDF" issue above (the report-generation code path fetching org branding independently/differently from the web-rendering code path, and drifting out of sync over time as one path is updated and the other isn't). Flagging this as an **inferred risk, not a directly sourced complaint** — recommend FLS explicitly share a single "get org branding for rendering" code path between the web UI and the PDF generator to avoid this class of bug by construction, rather than relying on two independent implementations staying in sync.

## 4. Configuration model: where branding lives, and snapshot-vs-live

**Where branding is configured (per product, as documented):**

- **Docebo**: branding is a platform/domain-level setting (White Label app), with a separate mechanism (Multi Domain / Extended Enterprise) for genuinely distinct per-audience branding on different domains of the same instance — i.e., branding lives at "domain" granularity, not per-report. [Docebo Multi Domain](https://www.docebo.com/products/learn-lms/features/multiple-domains/)
- **TalentLMS**: two levels — portal-wide white-label settings, and **per-branch** branding (logo, theme, catalog) where a branch is effectively a sub-tenant. This is the closest documented analogue to FLS's Site → Organisation hierarchy: TalentLMS branches let one paying platform operator present a different logo/theme to each of *its* downstream clients, same as FLS Organisations sitting under a Site. [TalentLMS Branches](https://www.talentlms.com/features/branches)
- **Open edX**: supports "organization dedicated certificate template" — i.e., the certificate template/branding is explicitly keyed to the course-providing organization, not just the platform. This directly validates the FLS design direction of keying report branding to Organisation rather than Site. [Open edX: organization dedicated certificate template](https://discuss.openedx.org/t/organization-dedicated-certificate-template/1442)
- No vendor's public docs (from the sources found) described a **per-report override** (i.e., "use a different logo for this one specific report run") — branding configuration in every documented case lives at an org/branch/domain record, not at the individual report/document level. This suggests FLS should likewise treat org branding as a property of the Organisation record, not something re-specified at report-generation time, unless there's a specific reason (e.g., historical accuracy — see snapshot question below) to override it.
- **No vendor was found to document an explicit "use platform branding instead of org branding for this report" opt-out.** This appears to be an uncommon feature in the market — most systems assume if an org has its own branding configured, it always applies. Worth treating as an open product question for FLS rather than assuming it's expected (see Implications section).

**Snapshot-at-generation vs. read-live-at-render:**

- No LMS-specific vendor documentation was found directly addressing whether certificate/report branding is snapshotted at generation time or re-read live on each view/re-download.
- The closest, well-established precedent is **invoicing systems**, where the dominant and expected pattern is to **snapshot branding (and other invoice-defining data) at generation time**, precisely so that historical invoices remain visually/factually accurate as of when they were issued, even if the vendor's/customer's branding changes later. Generic guidance on building invoice-PDF generators explicitly discusses fetching/embedding logo assets **at generation time** (with associated concerns like URL validation, fetch timeouts, and SSRF protection when logos come from an external URL) rather than re-fetching on every view. [How I Built a PDF Invoice Generation API](https://dev.to/jarachagent/how-i-built-a-pdf-invoice-generation-api-and-what-i-learned-about-pdf-rendering-3mpp)
- The general software pattern (and the reasoning behind it) is that a **generated document is treated as a record of a point in time** — re-rendering it later with new branding would be surprising/undesirable for anything that has record/audit character (which a cohort progress report plausibly does, especially in a compliance-adjacent training context — see Q6). No direct LMS complaint thread was found about "re-downloaded a report and the logo had changed," but this is a predictable consequence of a live-render (non-snapshotted) design, and the invoicing-industry norm of snapshotting exists specifically to avoid that class of surprise.
- **Recommendation-relevant tension**: if FLS snapshots at generation time, then correcting a wrong/blurry logo requires regenerating past reports (or accepting they stay wrong) — this is a real product trade-off worth surfacing to the user explicitly rather than deciding silently.

## 5. Multi-brand edge cases: platform's own default/house organisation

- No vendor documentation was found describing explicit handling of "the organisation IS the platform vendor itself" (i.e., no real customer org, so org branding == platform branding). This looks like a genuine gap in publicly available material, not just an under-documented feature — plausibly because most of the researched hosted SaaS LMS vendors (Docebo, TalentLMS, Thinkific, Teachable) are pure platform-providers where **every customer account is, by definition, a real distinct organisation**; there is no "house" org using the platform's own reports for itself in the same way FLS's demo/default org might.
- This is architecturally closer to FLS's situation only in the **self-hosted, single-installer** products (Moodle, Open edX, LearnDash) — but even there, no explicit "suppress duplicate powered-by when org == platform" logic was found documented; self-hosted products generally don't have a "powered by the platform" mark to begin with (see Q1), so the duplication problem doesn't arise for them either.
- **Conclusion: this is an open design question with no clear existing-market precedent to lean on.** FLS will likely need to originate its own convention here rather than copy one. The most defensible approach, by analogy to how "N/A" or empty-state fields are typically handled elsewhere (avoid showing two nearly-identical brand marks), would be to detect when the report's Organisation is the platform's own house/default organisation and simply **not render the "Powered by X" line a second time** (since the primary brand already *is* the platform) — but this is a recommendation, not something confirmed elsewhere.

## 6. Legal / trust angle (brief)

- In vocational-training-adjacent regulated contexts, there is a real precedent for **requiring** identification of the issuing body on training records. In Australia, **ASQA's Integrity of Nationally Recognised Training Practice Guide** requires Registered Training Organisations (RTOs) to comply with the AQF Qualifications Issuance Policy, which mandates that AQF certification documentation **include the name, registration code, and logo of the issuing organisation**. Separately, ASQA restricts use of the "Nationally Recognised Training" logo to accredited training contexts. [ASQA: Integrity of Nationally Recognised Training Practice Guide](https://www.asqa.gov.au/for-providers/standards-for-RTOs/practice-guides/compliance-standards-for-rtos/integrity-of-nationally-recognised-training-practice-guide)
- This establishes a **general principle relevant to FLS's platform mark question**: in compliance-adjacent training contexts, regulators care about the *issuing organisation* being clearly identified on the document — this maps directly to the Organisation branding being primary, which is exactly the FLS direction. It does **not** establish that the *software platform/vendor* needs to be identified for regulatory purposes — that requirement, where it exists, is about the training organisation, not the LMS vendor.
- More broadly, general software/data provenance and audit-trail literature (outside the LMS/training space specifically) emphasizes that auditors/regulators need to be able to **locate and confirm the authenticity/provenance of evidence** — this is a generic argument for *some* form of "this record was produced by system X, version Y, at time Z" provenance metadata (which could be a visible mark, an invisible PDF metadata field, or both), but is not LMS-specific and was not found tied to any concrete regulatory requirement mandating a *visible* platform mark. [General provenance/audit-trail guidance](https://www.suralink.com/blog/audit-trail-software)
- **Net assessment**: there is a plausible, non-trivial argument that keeping a small, non-removable "generated by FLS" provenance marker (even if visually minimized) has genuine value for auditability/authenticity of records that end up in front of a regulator or auditor — distinct from, and a stronger argument than, generic vendor-attribution marketing. This is worth surfacing explicitly to the user as a reason to consider making the platform mark **always-present** (even if minimized) rather than fully suppressible, though this is a recommendation to weigh, not a hard requirement found in any FLS-relevant regulation.

## Implications for FLS

1. **Key org branding to the Organisation record, not to a per-report override.** No researched product supports a per-report branding override; every precedent (TalentLMS branches, Open edX org-dedicated certificate templates, Docebo multi-domain) keys branding to the org/tenant record. Keep it simple: one branding config per Organisation, reused by every report for that org.
2. **Share one branding-resolution code path between the live web UI and the PDF generator.** The most plausible root cause of "branding shows on screen but not on the exported report" (a real, well-evidenced bug class in adjacent products) is two independent implementations drifting apart. Have the PDF renderer call the exact same "resolve org branding" function/service as the web UI, not a separate lookup.
3. **Treat PDF report generation as tenant-context-sensitive, especially if async.** Cross-tenant branding leaks in SaaS are commonly rooted in background jobs losing tenant context. If FLS generates PDFs via a Celery/async task, explicitly pass and re-validate the Organisation (and Site) on the job, don't rely on ambient request context.
4. **Require/prefer vector (SVG) org logos, or set a documented minimum raster resolution, and test transparency-on-colour-band rendering explicitly.** Blurry/stretched logos and transparent-PNG-halo artifacts are two of the best-evidenced complaint classes in this research; both are cheap to mitigate up front with upload validation (accept SVG, or enforce min-resolution + reject obviously-too-small raster uploads) and a rendering test case with a transparent PNG on the org's own brand-color header band.
5. **Cap or gracefully handle long organisation names in the report layout** — either via a sensible max-length validation at Organisation-name entry time (cheap, matches the X.509 64-char precedent) and/or dynamic font-sizing in the PDF template, rather than a fixed-width text box that can silently truncate or overflow.
6. **Decide, and document, whether branding is snapshotted at report-generation time or read live.** Industry precedent (invoicing) strongly favors **snapshotting at generation time** for anything with record/audit character — which cohort progress reports plausibly have (see point 8). Recommend FLS default to snapshot-at-generation, and treat "the org logo changed after this report was generated" as an accepted, expected trade-off (old reports keep the old logo) rather than a bug — but this should be an explicit, documented decision, not an accident of implementation.
7. **Decide explicitly whether "Powered by FLS" is ever fully suppressible, and if so, at what level.** Every comparable commercial product with a "powered by" mark makes it removable (via tier or config toggle) — but FLS is not selling tiers to platform operators in the same way, and there's a genuine, sourced argument (Q6) that keeping a minimal always-on platform mark supports auditability/provenance for compliance-adjacent training records. Recommend defaulting to **always show a minimized "Powered by FLS" mark**, non-suppressible, unless the user has a specific reason (e.g., contractual/enterprise white-label commitment) to make it configurable.
8. **Handle the "organisation IS the platform's own house/default org" case explicitly** — no market precedent exists to copy, but the sensible default (by analogy with avoiding duplicate near-identical brand marks) is to suppress the second/redundant "Powered by FLS" line when the report's Organisation is FLS's own default/house organisation, since the primary brand already is the platform.
9. **Consider treating the platform attribution as provenance metadata, not just a visible mark** — e.g., embedding "Generated by FreedomLS vX.Y at <timestamp>" in PDF document metadata (invisible) in addition to (or even instead of, if the user chooses full suppressibility) a visible footer mark, giving auditability without a mandatory visual footprint. This directly addresses the Q6 legal/trust argument with less UX cost than an always-visible mark.
10. **Explicitly test the "logo shows in preview/web UI but breaks in the actual generated PDF" scenario** as an acceptance-test case, given how common and quiet this failure mode is in adjacent products (image path/URL resolution differing between the live web renderer and the PDF-generation renderer, especially for file-field-backed vs. inline-asset-backed logo storage).

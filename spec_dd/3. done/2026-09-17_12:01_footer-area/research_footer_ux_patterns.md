# Research: footer UX patterns for learner-facing LMS pages with a docked sidebar

## 1. How comparable products handle footers on in-app course/lesson pages

Verifiable public documentation vs behaviour I could only infer is called out per product.

- **Moodle 4.x** — Verified via MoodleDocs. The course page layout (theme Boost, `drawers.php`) uses **sliding side panels** (course index on the left, blocks drawer on the right) that overlay/dock independently of the main content column, *not* a single full-height sticky sidebar baked into the grid. Moodle additionally has a documented **footer block area on the course page itself** — footer content is a *block region*, i.e. it is explicitly scoped to sit under the content column, not full-bleed under the drawers. Admins can also inject sitewide footer HTML via "Before BODY is closed." This is the closest existing-product precedent for "footer scoped to content, sidebar is a separate overlay/drawer system." [Header and footer — MoodleDocs](https://docs.moodle.org/502/en/Header_and_footer), [Course homepage — MoodleDocs](https://docs.moodle.org/502/en/Course_homepage), [Moodle 4.0 developer update](https://moodledev.io/docs/4.1/devupdate)
- **Canvas LMS** — General product-marketing sources describe Canvas's course-page UI as clean and mobile-adaptive, but none of the results gave verifiable, citable detail on footer placement relative to its course sidebar. **Inferred, not verified**: Canvas's course navigation sidebar is not a docked full-viewport-height column in the way FLS's is, so this is not a reusable precedent for FLS's specific structural problem.
- **Coursera** — Could not verify in-product footer placement on the course-player page itself; search results surfaced only marketing/legal pages (Terms of Use, learner guides), not the authenticated player UI. **Not verified** — do not treat any claim about Coursera's player footer as fact.
- **edX / Docebo / Teachable / Thinkific** — No citable, specific findings on in-app footer placement for sidebar course pages; general web knowledge (not independently verified here) is that most course-player/lesson UIs in this class of product ship **no footer at all** inside the player, treating the player as a focused task view, with any footer content pushed to a separate "browse courses" / dashboard shell. Treat this as an *inference from category convention*, not a cited fact.
- **Documentation-style sidebar apps (Docusaurus, GitBook, ReadTheDocs)** — Verified: both Docusaurus and GitBook treat the footer as a **first-class themeable region configured centrally** (`docusaurus.config.js` footer key; GitBook's footer customization with logo/link sections), and it renders as its own full-width component **below the whole page**, not scoped to the content column. This works for them because their sidebar (docs TOC) is not sticky-docked to full 100vh — it scrolls with the page and is normally shorter than the content, so reaching the footer doesn't require scrolling past a whole screen of pinned chrome first. This is a **materially different structural situation** from FLS's `_base_interface.html`, where the sidebar is `position: sticky; height: calc(100vh - var(--sidebar-top))`. [Styling and Layout — Docusaurus](https://docusaurus.io/docs/styling-layout), [Layout and structure — GitBook Documentation](https://gitbook.com/docs/publishing-documentation/customization/layout-and-structure)
- **ReadTheDocs** — no citable specific finding surfaced; not verified.

**Takeaway**: the one verifiable precedent that actually matches FLS's shape (sticky/docked sidebar not present, footer scoped to content) is Moodle's footer-as-block-region. The docs-site products are a false-friend precedent — they do put a full footer under a sidebar layout, but their sidebar doesn't create the "whole viewport of pinned chrome to scroll past" problem FLS has.

## 2. "App chrome vs marketing chrome" footer pattern

This is a well-established, citable pattern, not FLS-specific:

- Nielsen Norman Group: footers are "a cost-free addition to the user experience" for essentially all page types, **except** where infinite scroll (or, by extension, a persistent full-viewport panel) removes any "consistent bottom." Their prescribed fix for that case is not "no footer" but a **"mini footer within the right rail"** — i.e., move the reduced footer content out of the page-bottom flow and into the persistent side chrome instead. [Web Page Footers 101 — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/)
- NN/g also documents **contextual/role-aware footers** (their Medium.com example: footer content differs for subscribers vs non-subscribers), establishing that varying footer content by authentication state is an accepted pattern, not a hack. [Web Page Footers 101 — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/)
- Eleken's 2026 footer-pattern guide states the split directly: **"In SaaS products... we often skip the footer in product UIs and redistribute key links like legal, help, or docs into settings menus or support flyouts."** The driver is that authenticated, task-focused product UI treats persistent chrome (sidebar/nav) as the wayfinding surface, and a footer at the bottom of a long, possibly-scrolled page is a weak, easily-missed place to put compliance/support links compared to a menu that's always reachable. [10 modern footer UX patterns for 2026 — Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/footer-ux)

Where products draw the line, consistently: **marketing, dashboard-of-finite-cards, and account/settings pages get the full footer; the deep, narrow, task-focused "doing the work" screens (editor, player, wizard, checkout) get a reduced or no footer**, because on those screens the footer competes with, rather than complements, the primary task chrome.

## 3. Known complaints / failure modes for this footer shape

- **Unreachable-behind-scroll footers.** Directly the FLS structural problem described: with a `position: sticky` full-height sidebar, a learner has to scroll an entire extra viewport of already-fully-visible sidebar content past before the footer appears, for no informational gain — the sidebar isn't revealing anything new as it "scrolls," it's already fully on-screen.
- **Sticky-sidebar-vs-footer collision.** Widely documented as a generic CSS/UX problem: sticky-positioned elements are commonly implemented without a defined "release" point, so they either overlap the footer or the footer has to add explicit `position: relative` / spacing hacks to escape it. Real-world fixes cited include forcing the footer into its own stacking context and constraining the sticky element's containing block so it stops before the footer starts. [Sticky footer but problem with overlapping — GeneratePress forum](https://generatepress.com/forums/topic/sticky-footer-but-problem-with-overlapping/), [Problem: Sticky Sidebar Navigation — Educative](https://www.educative.io/courses/css-design-patterns/sticky-sidebar-navigation)
- **Duplicated navigation.** A footer with "Courses / Dashboard / Help" links directly under a sidebar that already lists those same destinations is redundant chrome — it adds scroll distance without adding information, and risks the two navigation surfaces drifting out of sync over time.
- **Infinite-scroll / virtualised "dead zone."** NN/g's explicit point: content that never has a "final scroll position" makes a bottom-anchored footer structurally unreachable, which is why they recommend relocating footer content into persistent chrome instead. [Web Page Footers 101 — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/)
- **Mobile footer pushed far below the fold.** On the FLS `_base_interface.html` shell specifically, below `lg` the sidebar becomes a modal overlay rather than an in-flow column (`_base_interface.html` lines 43–111), so on mobile the footer question is different from desktop: there's no docked column to scroll past, but the content column itself is often long (topic content + activities), so a full marketing-style footer still adds meaningful scroll distance for little payoff.
- **Collision with a genuine sticky bottom bar.** This is exactly what FLS's exam/form runner already has. `learner_interface/templates/learner_interface/course_form_page.html` builds a `h-screen flex flex-col overflow-hidden` layout with a `flex-none sticky bottom-0` action bar (Previous / Next / Submit) pinned to the viewport bottom (lines 73, 316). A generic page footer rendered into that same document would either be invisible (clipped by `overflow-hidden`) or would visually stack beneath/behind the runner's own sticky bar — a direct instance of the "footer fights sticky bottom bar" failure mode called out in the brief.

## 4. Layout options for a footer under a docked-sidebar grid

Given the settled seam — `{% block footer %}` in `freedom_ls/base/templates/_base.html`, placed after `</main>` (line 117), wrapping the shipped `partials/footer.html` — here is what each layout option costs, and whether it's reachable from that seam alone or requires touching `_base_interface.html`:

| Option | Description | Reachable from `{% block footer %}` after `</main>` as-is? |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-bleed under both columns** | Default behaviour: footer renders full page-width, below the entire `.side-panel-grid` (sidebar + content), because it sits outside `<main>` entirely. | **Yes** — this is what happens with zero extra work. It is also the shape that produces the described structural problem on `_base_interface.html` pages. |
| **Constrained to the content column only** (aligned with the content well, not under the sidebar track) | Footer starts at the same horizontal offset as the right-hand content column. | **No, not cleanly.** `partials/footer.html` renders after `</main>`, outside `.side-panel-grid`, so it has no access to `--sidebar-width`/`--sidebar-top` or the grid's column definitions except by duplicating them. Doing this without duplication means the footer needs to *live inside* the same grid container — i.e. restructuring `_base_interface.html`. |
| **Rendered inside the scroll container** (i.e. inside `#interface-main`, after `{% block content %}`, so it scrolls with the content column and sits directly under topic content, at the content column's own scroll position) | Footer becomes part of the per-topic content flow rather than page-bottom-of-everything. | **No.** Requires `_base_interface.html` to blank `{% block footer %}` (as `_exam_runner_base.html` already does for `{% block header %}`, line 29-31) and instead render a footer partial itself inside its own `{% block body %}`, after `{% block content %}` (around line 243). This is a template restructuring, not a seam usage. |
| **Reduced / compact variant** | Same full-bleed position (after `</main>`), but a shorter, single-line footer partial swapped in for this shell. | **Yes** — `_base_interface.html` (or any shell) can override `{% block footer %}` to include a different, reduced partial while keeping the existing after-`</main>` position. No grid restructuring needed. |
| **Suppressed entirely** | No footer rendered for this shell. | **Yes** — trivially reachable by overriding `{% block footer %}` with an empty block, exactly mirroring how `_exam_runner_base.html` already blanks `{% block header %}` (lines 29-31) because the runner owns its own top bar. |

The practical implication: **the seam as designed cheaply supports "full," "reduced," and "off."** Getting a footer that is properly aligned to the content column, or that lives inside the sidebar shell's own scroll container, is real extra work belonging to `_base_interface.html`'s own layout, not something a downstream project gets for free just by overriding `partials/footer.html`.

## 5. Reduced-footer content pattern

When products do ship a compact footer on in-app/authenticated pages, the recurring pattern (per the design-system/footer-pattern sources above) is a single horizontal row containing only:

- Copyright / org name (`© {{ year }} {{ org }}`)
- 2-3 compliance-required links max (Privacy, Terms, and — where relevant — Accessibility)
- Optionally a version/build indicator

Sources explicitly describe this as: logo/name left, meta info right, "three links max," reduced type size (`text-xs`), reduced vertical padding (`py-4` vs `py-12` for a full marketing footer), and no additional navigation, CTAs, or branding flourishes. [10 modern footer UX patterns for 2026 — Eleken](https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/footer-ux); general compact-footer guidance corroborated by [Minimal Footer patterns](https://www.shadcn.io/blocks/footer-minimal-01) and [Web Page Footers 101 — NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/footers/).

## 6. Recommendation for FLS

**(a) Plain `_base.html` pages — dashboard, course detail, all-courses, legal docs, auth pages: render the full footer.**
These are finite-length, no docked full-height sidebar competes for the viewport, and this is exactly the class of page (dashboard/account/marketing-adjacent) every cited source agrees gets the full footer; legal docs and auth pages in particular benefit from footer-hosted compliance/contact links.

**(b) `_base_interface.html` pages — course topics, educator interface: suppress the footer block for this shell (override `{% block footer %}` empty, same pattern the runner already uses for `{% block header %}`).**
The structural problem in the brief is real and unsolved by the plain seam (§4): a full-bleed footer here forces scrolling a whole viewport of already-visible, pinned sidebar for no reason, and per §2/§3 (NN/g, Eleken) task-focused authenticated UI with persistent nav chrome is exactly the case where footer content is conventionally dropped or relocated, not force-fit at page-bottom. If compliance requires footer content site-wide, that's the one case worth the real work in §4 (footer scoped inside `#interface-main`/content column) — flag it as a follow-up decision, don't default to full-bleed here.

**(c) The exam/form runner (`_exam_runner_base.html` / `course_form_page.html`): suppress entirely.**
This shell is already `h-screen overflow-hidden` with its own `sticky bottom-0` action bar (Previous/Next/Submit) — a second footer is either clipped by `overflow-hidden` or collides with the runner's own sticky bar, the exact failure mode called out in the brief (§3). This mirrors how the runner already blanks `{% block header %}` because it owns its own top bar.
*Note for the human*: `course_applications/form_page.html` extends `_base.html` directly, not `_exam_runner_base.html` — it has no sticky footer of its own, so the collision argument doesn't apply there; it will get the default full footer like any other plain page unless deliberately suppressed. Worth a conscious call, not an oversight.

**(d) Error pages (400/403/404/429/500/503): suppress, and note two are structurally unreachable anyway.**
`500.html` and `503.html` do **not** extend `_base.html` — they are standalone documents with their own `<html>/<head>/<body>` (per their own comments: they must render without any request/DB context), so `{% block footer %}` cannot reach them at all without separately hardcoding footer markup into those two files. `400.html`, `403.html`, `403_csrf.html`, `404.html`, and `429.html` **do** extend `_base.html` and would get the footer by default. For consistency across the whole error-page family, and because these are minimal, centered, single-recovery-action interstitials (`flex min-h-screen flex-col justify-center` with one `<c-error-page>` and 1-2 buttons) — recommend suppressing the footer on the reachable five too, so all six error pages behave the same rather than five having a footer and two structurally not.

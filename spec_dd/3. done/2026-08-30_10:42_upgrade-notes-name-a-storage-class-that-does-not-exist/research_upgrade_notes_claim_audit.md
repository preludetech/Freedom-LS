# Claim-by-claim audit: `upgrade_notes.md` vs HEAD of `main`

Audited: `spec_dd/3. done/2026-08-27_12:32_prod_bucket_setup/upgrade_notes.md` (118 lines) against
`config/settings_base.py`, `config/settings_prod.py`, `freedom_ls/deployment/storage.py`,
`freedom_ls/deployment/checks.py`, `freedom_ls/reports/models.py`, `freedom_ls/reports/checks.py`,

## Verdict

The document's structural claims (what changed, why, the bucket-sharing design, the migration names,
the report-path prefix) are almost all still true — the underlying design the notes describe is the
one that shipped. But the document is stale in one specific way that recurs throughout: it was written
against an earlier state of `freedom_ls_deployment.checks` that had only two error checks (`E001`,
`E002`) and a `public`-alias implementation the notes call `OverwritingFileSystemStorage`. Two more
checks (`E003`, `E004`) landed afterward and are not mentioned anywhere in the document, which makes
every claim that enumerates "two errors" or a fixed error count wrong or incomplete. Layered on top of
that is a set of unrelated numeric slips (three different, undefined "alias count" conventions used
inconsistently, one paragraph that contradicts itself in its own two sentences) and one factually wrong
exception-type claim independent of the known-bad class-name issue.

Count: **11 inaccurate/incomplete claims** found beyond the three already-confirmed known-bad ones
(which are not re-litigated below, per instructions), plus **1 non-numeric ambiguous claim** flagged
separately. Roughly half the document (migration names, report-path prefix, bucket-sharing pairs,
frontmatter alias defaults, the general shape of E001/E002) checks out exactly as written.

# Research: known complaints (bundle downstream_bugs_oct_29)

Basis: the 15 idea.md files in the bundle (read in full) plus general LMS and Django-app experience. The QA report glob showed `system_qa/<NN>_*/qa_report.md` folders (00 to 05+) rather than one mega report, and the ideas already quote their QA references, so I did not re-read the per-area reports. Web-sourced items carry URLs. Items marked (judgement) are my own inference, not sourced.

## 1. Learner complaints

- Losing place: "Continue" restarts at item 1 (judgement; the commonest "Continue" complaint in any course product). Implication: `course_home` target for in-progress learners only. "Start course" and "Review course" stay on item 1. The `data-forward-path` back link must use the same URL.
- Losing answers on exit: "it said saved, it wasn't" is worse than no promise (judgement). Implication: "Leave and save" must POST the current page with `require_answers=False` plus `formnovalidate`. Otherwise remove "Your progress is saved" from the dialog. Pick one; do not ship neither.
- Confusing "Form", "test" and "quiz" naming (judgement). Implication: one word per strategy (quiz for `QUIZ`, neutral otherwise), sentence case, in all 6 places: `utils.py:755,765,772`, `course_form_page.html:80,107`, `course_form_complete.html:243`, `course_finish.html:64`. "Form" is the project's internal term and must not reach learners.
- Bounced with no explanation (LEARN-12), and the quiz result for a never-attempted quiz (LEARN-7). Implication: a redirect needs a visible reason. The LEARN-7 redirect to the start page is silent, which is fine because the start page explains itself. The LEARN-12 `messages.info` must land on a page that renders messages (see section 4).
- Stale "Your applications" rows after being registered (APP-5). Implication: a registered learner seeing "Pending review" plus "Continue application" reads as being lost. Hide the row (the idea's choice) or show "Accepted". Never leave an editable form on a granted course.
- Free-course acquisition copy ("One click. No credit card.") shown to someone already enrolled or finished. Implication: post-funnel copy such as "You're enrolled", with the real price and enrolment summary kept.
- US date formats in a non-US product (`F j, Y` on the finish page vs `j M Y` elsewhere, LEARN-17). Implication: reuse the project's existing `j M Y` format. Month-name formats avoid the day/month ambiguity that all-numeric formats cause.
- Raw LaTeX or Mermaid source showing (LEARN-1, LEARN-6). Implication: raw source reads as a broken product. Even if Mermaid is not supported, the learner should not see unformatted fence text. Decide: render, or warn at `content_validate`.
- Sideways page scroll on phones from a wide `pre` (LEARN-6). Implication: it is the page that scrolls, not the block. Fix with `overflow-x: auto; max-width: 100%` on rendered code. Check that nothing in a flex or grid parent keeps a `min-width` that defeats it (needs `min-w-0`).
- Two h1s and no skip link. WCAG 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks (Level A) requires a mechanism to skip repeated blocks. A skip link is the usual technique. WCAG does not forbid multiple h1s, but screen-reader heading navigation starting at the site name is a documented usability nuisance. Sources: https://wcag.dock.codes/documentation/wcag-success-criteria/wcag241/ , https://docs.acquia.com/web-governance/bypass-blocks . Implication: the skip link is the compliance item. The `<span>` change is a usability improvement. The skip link target needs `id` on `<main>` and `tabindex="-1"` so focus actually moves in all browsers (judgement).
- Honeypot false positives. Password managers and autofill do fill hidden fields. Reports: 1Password, LastPass and Bitwarden filling all fields (https://dev.to/alexisfranorge/why-your-honeypot-catches-humans-not-bots-3bcd , secondary, unverified); TYPO3 documents it as rare but real (https://docs.typo3.org/p/davidkohr/ke-search-honeypot/14.0/en-us/_sources/KnownProblems/Index.rst.txt); one Perch CMS poster estimated about 20% of a spam tab were false positives (https://community.perchcms.com/forum/thread/366-forms-honeypot-false-positives/); Firefox address autofill filled a hidden-container honeypot where Chrome and Edge did not (https://support.mozilla.org/km/questions/1279049). `autocomplete="off"` is widely ignored by password managers. Implication: the field name matters (`fax_number` is an address-autofill-type name, which makes a trip more likely; a non-semantic name is safer). The "type by hand" advice only works if the widget never re-renders the value (`format_value` returns `None`). Also keep a non-blocking path: do not permanently lock out someone who trips it.
- Email confirmation link clicked twice, or opened on another device (ACCT-6, ACCT-8). Implication: a verified learner seeing "expired or invalid" thinks the account is broken. A verified address needs "already confirmed" plus a link on. Loss of `next` on a second device is an expected, common case (mail on phone, signup on desktop). At minimum tell the learner where to go. Storing `next` server-side is the larger change; keep it out of scope unless cheap.
- Phishing-looking "confirm your email" for an address you did not add (ACCT-9). Implication: the mail must say the address was added to an account and to ignore it if that was not them. The sender name and the action wording must be identical across the ACCT-9 and ACCT-7 copy edits.
- Stock-template wording (ACCT-4, ACCT-7): "Bad Token", "Sign Out" next to "Log in", Title Case against sentence case, US spelling "finalize". Implication: one vocabulary (Log in/Log out), sentence case, UK spelling, applied to every overridden template.
- Misleading UI states: "Coming soon" with a tick (DISC-9), a promise of a notification nothing sends (DISC-1), the time limit labelled "Course" (LEARN-13), outline chevrons pointing the wrong way (LEARN-14). Implication: each is a trust issue; keep the fixes small and consistent with the existing accordion icons.
- Non-field errors without `role="alert"` and field errors rendered under the wrong field (ACCT-3). Implication: screen-reader users get no feedback on a failed signup. Match login's `role="alert"` pattern, put each error with its own field, and add name autocomplete tokens.

## 2. Downstream integrator complaints

Typical complaints about a reusable Django app (mostly judgement and experience; the evidence here is the First-Class bundle itself):

- Tests that assume the app's own default settings. Evidence: `test_svg_has_intrinsic_width_and_height` (hard-coded 24) and `test_sidebar_lists_the_six_sections...` (assumes `MESSAGING_ENABLED=True`); the second also exposes the three `TestMessagesButton` tests. The app's CI uses its own settings, so the breakage is invisible to the app. Implication: pin each setting the test depends on, cover both branches, and prefer asserting the invariant over the literal. State in the upgrade notes that app tests rendering a real template fail when the downstream shadows it. That is intended drift detection, not a bug.
- Tests that render templates the downstream shadows (the `TestMessagesButton` case). Implication: either mark such tests with the existing `fls_internal` marker (only if the behaviour is genuinely FLS-only) or document the failure mode. The `fls_internal` marker is the wrong choice for portable behaviour, as both test ideas already conclude.
- Hard-coded copy and `<title>` branding (DISC-5, DISC-6, DISC-1, `COURSE_DEADLINE_TITLE`, `_COMING_SOON_ACQUISITION_SUBTEXT`). Implication: a copy change is a breaking change for anyone who matched the old string or translated it. Document changed strings in the upgrade notes. The meta-description fallback needs a setting or `SiteConfig` field. A context processor workaround fails FLS's own `test_og_description_falls_back_to_the_generic_default` in the downstream suite.
- Templates that cannot be partially overridden, so one line forces a whole-template shadow that then drifts. Evidence: `header_bar.html` (h1), `login_prompt.html` (next), `course_detail.html` (Continue URL). Implication: a shadow silently misses every later FLS change to that file. Prefer template tags, `{% block %}` or `{% include %}` points, and context variables over shadows.
- Feature flags that silence the UI but not the data writers. Evidence: `NOTIFICATIONS_ENABLED` read only by the bell tag, so rows pile up at `/notifications/`. Implication: gate at the writer and the views. Note the downstream workaround `NOTIFICATION_CATEGORIES = []`, which must keep working.
- Acquisition copy baked into access backends. Evidence: the registered branch of `_free_access_decision` and `ApplicationCourseAccessBackend._ungated_decision`. Implication: backends should return structured state (enrolled or not, gated or not) and let the copy be chosen in one place. A downstream subclass should not need to rewrite strings.
- A fix reachable only by subclassing for one of two call sites (honeypot: the downstream swapped the signup widget but could not reach the application forms). Implication: put the fix in the shared widget.

Workaround inventory and what FLS must guarantee so it can be deleted safely. After deleting, the downstream must be able to delete without any code change other than removing the override and the settings pointer.

- `apps/project_setup/forms.py` (`BlankHoneypotInput`, `SignupForm`, `ACCOUNT_FORMS`). Cost: a shadowed signup form that drifts from `accounts.forms.SignupForm` (policy, name fields, autocomplete). Guarantee: `HoneypotInput` never renders a value, for `SignupForm`, `ApplicantDetailsForm` and `ApplicantEmailForm`. The `ACCOUNT_FORMS` setting must keep working with FLS's default form, so removing the entry is enough. Do not rename `honeypot_field()`, the field name or the error message in the same change.
- `apps/project_setup/course_access.py` (backend subclass, `COURSE_ACCESS_BACKEND`). Cost: copy replacement on any `can_access_content` decision, plus filtering the panel; it re-implements two FLS behaviours. Guarantee: `ApplicationCourseAccessBackend` (and the free backend) return post-funnel copy for registered learners, `is_accessible_for_free` stays correct for gated courses, and `get_active_applications` excludes registered courses. Keep the class path `course_applications.backends.ApplicationCourseAccessBackend` and the `CourseAccessDecision` field names. The downstream can then repoint `COURSE_ACCESS_BACKEND` to the stock class.
- `apps/project_setup/templatetags/auth_link_tags.py` (`url_back_here`) plus shadowed `login_prompt.html`. Cost: the shadow must track FLS markup. Guarantee: the stock `partials/login_prompt.html` already links back to the current page, without changing `url_with_next`'s pass-through (the signup form's in-form links depend on it). The downstream template tag is then unused and can be deleted. FLS keeps the tag name `url_with_next` and does not need to adopt `url_back_here`. Whatever new tag or flag FLS adds, say so in the upgrade notes.
- Shadowed `header_bar.html`. Guarantee: the stock file renders the site name in a `<span>` with the same classes and keeps `{% messages_button %}`, the bell tag, and every context variable the shadow consumes (`header_title`). The `TestMessagesButton` tests then pass without the shadow.
- Shadowed `course_detail.html`. Guarantee: `start_url` is the resume URL for "Continue" (`course_home`); the context names `start_url` and `cta_label` are unchanged. The shadow introduced `resume_url`, which FLS need not provide.
- `pyproject.toml` deselects (`test_svg_has_intrinsic_width_and_height`, three `test_settings_defaults.py`). Guarantee: the icon test passes under any `FREEDOM_LS_ICON_SET`. The sidebar test (not deselected, because messaging is on there) passes under both messaging values. The `test_settings_defaults.py` deselects belong to another spec, so keep them.
- Not covered by a workaround: ACCT, APP-3/8/9, LEARN-8 (none exist downstream). Those are new FLS templates; the downstream project has no `templates/account/` overrides, so adding stock overrides will not collide, but a downstream that already shadows `account/*` will not receive them.

## 3. Content author complaints

- Silent acceptance of broken content: a zero-page form loads with no warning and traps learners at 98% (LEARN-5); `correct: null` on wrong options shows "Not marked" (LEARN-11); Markdown in a plain-text section description shows literal asterisks (DISC-3); Mermaid fences show as raw text (LEARN-6). General complaint: content is "validated" only when a learner hits it. Implication: `content_validate` and `content_save` should fail or warn at authoring time. The idea sets zero-page forms as an error, and `content_save` must agree with `content_validate` or authors see different verdicts.
- What a good validation message contains (judgement):
  - the file or directory path of the offending item;
  - the rule, in author words, not model names ("a quiz needs at least one page");
  - what to do ("add a page folder under `<path>`, or delete the quiz");
  - severity (error blocks the save, warning does not);
  - for a repeated problem, one line per item with a count, not a stack trace.
- `correct: null` handling: normalise at `content_save`, or warn, when the question has at least one `correct: true` option. Do not silently rewrite author data without telling them.
- Markdown in a plain-text field: pick render (inline Markdown) or flag at validate. Whichever is chosen, say it in the content docs, otherwise authors keep guessing.
- Mermaid: the decision to render, warn or ignore must appear in the authoring docs, including how an unsupported fence degrades.
- A fix that turns a former warning into an error stops the save of an existing content repo (judgement). Implication: list the new errors in the upgrade notes, and name any item that previously loaded.

## 4. Risks of the fixes themselves

- `sanitize=False` path in `render_markdown` (accordion fix). Risk: stored XSS if an author-controlled string reaches it unsanitised, for example via a `{% markdown %}` tag in a downstream template. Implication: sanitise the author source exactly once before the cotton pass, make the unsanitised path an internal function (not a template-tag argument), and test that `<script>` or `onclick` in author input inside an accordion is still removed. Alternative 2 in the idea (render nested Markdown before the cotton pass) avoids the unsafe parameter entirely.
- `first_name` required on the profile form. Risk: sites whose signup policy does not require names get a form that rejects a blank name for users who signed up without one, and blocks an unrelated edit such as changing the last name. Implication: reuse `apply_signup_name_policy` with the request's policy, so the required rule follows the policy rather than a new hard requirement. Existing users with a blank name should not be blocked from saving only the other field if the policy does not require it.
- Redirect after profile save: tests and Playwright flows that assert the re-rendered page body now follow a redirect. The message must survive.
- `next` on header links: risks are open redirect, loops and long URLs. Use Django's `url_has_allowed_host_and_scheme` (allauth does so for `next`), pass only the path, not an absolute URL with a host, include the query string (or drop it, deliberately), URL-encode it, and never emit it on `/accounts/` pages or the home page. Beware a 404 or error page, and pages behind POST-only URLs, where "come back here" is wrong. Also caching: the header is rendered per request, so any full-page cache keyed without the path would serve the wrong `next` (judgement).
- `hx-replace-url` on the pager. In htmx 2, the history mechanism snapshots the DOM and restores it on Back; when no snapshot exists htmx re-requests the URL with `HX-History-Restore-Request`, so the server must return a full page there (https://four.htmx.org/reference/attributes/hx-replace-url , https://htmx.org/attributes/hx-push-url/ , https://github.com/bigskysoftware/htmx/discussions/919). htmx 4 reportedly drops the snapshot cache (secondary source: https://blog.openreplay.com/htmx-4-0-release/). The project is on htmx 2. Implication: the replaced URL is a full-page URL (the idea notes `clamped_page` handles it), so a reload and a Back restore both work. Expect stale-panel reports after Back (the idea's target, "the panel is on page 1 again", changes to "the panel shows the cached page") and test with two panels on one page (the query parameter must be section-specific so they do not overwrite each other). The comment "Paging still never pushes a URL" in the template must be updated.
- Redirecting the quiz result page (never attempted): bookmarks to `/complete` for an unattempted quiz change from an odd page to the start page. Low risk, but Playwright tests that expected the odd page will fail. Keep the existing result page for any learner with a completed attempt.
- Hiding registered courses from "Your applications": applicants who expect a status (accepted or rejected) lose it. Implication: show "Accepted" or a link to the course rather than a bare disappearance if the panel is the only place the learner saw their application. When approve/reject lands later, this filter must be revisited (the docstring in `queries.py` already says so). Draft applications on a registered course must also not be deleted, only hidden.
- Changing "Form" wording: downstream Playwright selectors (`get_by_role("button", name="Start Form")`) and translated strings (`.po` entries keyed by the old msgid) silently stop matching. Implication: list every changed string in the upgrade notes, keep the same element ids and `data-testid` attributes, and grep the downstream repo for the old strings before merge. Playwright's name matching is case-insensitive substring by default, but sentence-case changes and renamed words still break exact matches.
- `NOTIFICATIONS_ENABLED` early return: a downstream expecting rows to accumulate for a later enablement loses that history; hidden writes also differ from "no bell". Implication: document it as a behaviour change; confirm tests that call `raise_notification` under default settings (for example `learner_progress/signals.py:159`) now need the setting on. 404ing the views breaks bookmarks and email links to `/notifications/`. The `NOTIFICATION_CATEGORIES = []` workaround must keep working.
- Changing `COURSE_DEADLINE_TITLE` from "Course" to "Course time limit": longer text in a dashboard card (wrapping at 390px), and tests or Playwright steps matching "Course 19 Oct 2026" break. Check the layout at phone width.
- `overflow-x: auto` on all `pre`: it also hits code in admin, educator views and Django debug pages if the rule is global. Scope it to the rendered-content wrapper (the markdown output class), not a bare `pre` selector. Inline `pre` inside tables and prose already styled by Tailwind typography may double-scroll. A tabbable scroll region needs `tabindex="0"` and a label for keyboard users (judgement). Test that long lines scroll and that the document width equals the viewport at 390px.
- Appending the site name to every `<title>` once in `_base.html`: pages that already append it (player pages via `_course_base.html:12`) get "X — Site — Site". Implication: remove the suffix from `_course_base.html` in the same change, and check every `head_title` override (articles, legal docs, login, signup, error pages) and any downstream template that already appends. The home page should be just the site name, not "Site — Site". Downstream tests matching `<title>` exactly will break.
- `messages.info` on a blocked-item redirect: the learner sees nothing if the target page has no messages region, then sees the message later on a different page (stale message). Implication: confirm the course detail template renders the messages block (the idea names the course detail page as the target), and add one test that asserts the message appears in the rendered redirect target.
- Phone minimum-digit rule: libphonenumber accepts national numbers from 2 digits (https://bird.com/explained/numbers/what-is-e164-phone-number-format), and short numbers exist in some territories, so a hard 7-digit floor can reject legitimate numbers. Sweden's plan gives 7 as the minimum there (https://www.pts.se/globalassets/globala-block/nummertillstand/the-swedish-numbering-plan-for-telephony-according-to-itu---2024-01-08.pdf). I did not find a country-by-country minimum list, so treat the threshold as a judgement call. Safe approach: count digits only after stripping spaces, hyphens, parentheses, dots and a leading `+`; require at least 5 digits (reject "hello" and obvious junk without rejecting the shortest widely used national lengths), accept up to 15 (the E.164 maximum), and allow only digits, spaces, `+`, `-`, `.`, `(`, `)` plus an optional extension marker such as `ext` or `x`. If the idea's 7 is kept, state it is a deliberate floor and make it a named constant so downstream can adjust it. The message must say what is expected (for example "Enter a phone number with at least 5 digits, such as +27 82 123 4567"). Never block on country-specific format until libphonenumber lands.
- Honeypot fix (`format_value` returns `None`): a legitimate extra change is that a bot-flagged form now re-renders blank, which is the intended outcome. Risk is low. A test must bind a filled value and assert no `value` attribute for all three forms.
- Overriding the remaining allauth templates: any downstream that already shadows `account/*` keeps its old copy and sees no change (expected). Downstream tests matching allauth's stock strings ("Sign Out", "Bad Token") will break. The `ACCOUNT_PREVENT_ENUMERATION` flow means the "already confirmed" message must not reveal whether someone else's address exists: show it only to the logged-in owner of that address.
- Registered-branch copy change: JSON-LD and the free badge read `is_accessible_for_free`. Keep it accurate for gated courses (False when a price or application applies) or search snippets will change. Check the sitemap and JSON-LD tests under the downstream settings.

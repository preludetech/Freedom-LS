# Spec: referral attribution over time

Inputs: `idea.md`, `answers.md` and the `research_*.md` files in this directory.

## Purpose

Attribution is captured once, at account signup. `SignupAttribution` is one row per user, written by
`record_attribution_on_signup` from a cookie frozen on the visitor's first tracked landing. A later
landing changes nothing: `AttributionCaptureMiddleware._site_to_mint_for` returns `None` as soon as a
valid cookie exists.

That says which code an account came from. It cannot say which code was in play when the person
applied for a course weeks later through a different partner's link. Referral payouts are coming, and
they need that answer recorded rather than reconstructed.

This work keeps a **timeline of touches per person**, and freezes the first and last referral code in
play at each attributable moment.

## Terminology

| Term | Status | Meaning / source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `AttributionTouch` | coined | One tracked landing recorded against one account. The app already calls a tracked landing a touch (`FirstTouchCount`, `first_touch_from_request`); this keeps every touch per person, in order. |
| `Conversion` | narrowed | One attributable moment for referral payouts: an account signup, or a learner-initiated course application, expression of interest or self-registration. A row in `referral_tracking`, reported nowhere; not the ad-platform conversion of `freedom_ls/google_tag/google_ads.py`. |
| **in play** | coined | Of a referral code at an instant: carried by an `AttributionTouch` for that user, on that site, at or before the instant, and no older than the attribution window. |
| **live touch** | coined | A touch written straight to a row because the visitor was signed in, as against one carried in the cookie and flushed later. |

A `Conversion` is not a registration. "Registration" stays the FLS word for course access.

## Scope

**In.**

- A per-user `AttributionTouch` timeline, fed three ways: the cookie flushed at signup, the cookie
  flushed at login, and live touches while signed in.
- A cookie that carries a bounded list of touches beside the frozen first touch.
- One `Conversion` row per attributable moment, holding the first and last codes in play, frozen.
- Hooks where a learner acts: signup, starting an application (including an anonymous application once
  it is attached to an account), expressing interest, self-registering.
- Read-only admin and CSV export over both models.
- A prune command for touches no conversion references.

**Out.**

- Payouts, commission, money, and anything partner-facing. No `Organisation` link on `ReferralCode`.
- A dashboard or conversion-rate view.
- A consent gate for the tracking cookie (see Open questions).
- Any change to `SignupAttribution`: its shape, append-only guard and read-only admin stay.
- Attributing staff-created registrations and cohort memberships.
- Touches for untagged landings from another site. `referral-tracking-external-referer-first-touch`
  adds its own touch kind, field and cookie key later.

## Decisions

- **This supersedes the `referral-links` rule** that a referral code "reaches attribution only as a
  first touch". Every later tagged landing now reaches it too.
- **The timeline is the capture; the frozen pair is the payout record.** Raw touches let a future
  credit rule be re-run over history. The frozen pair keeps a figure someone was paid on from moving
  when a touch is pruned or a rule changes.
- **No credit rule is built in.** A `Conversion` records `first_referral_code` and
  `last_referral_code` side by side. Whoever runs a payout picks first or last in the query.
- **Only referral codes are ever in play.** `first_touch` and `last_touch` are the earliest and latest
  touches with a non-blank `referral_code`. A UTM-only touch is recorded but never displaces a
  partner.
- **Both codes blank is a recorded answer.** It means no coded touch reached a row inside the window.
  A cookie that was lost or expired before signup looks the same; that is accepted.
- **Both models key on `user`, not `Learner`.** Attribution belongs to the account, and a signup
  happens before any `Learner` row exists.
- **Only learner-initiated moments convert.** An admin registering a learner or adding a cohort is
  staff work.
- **A reactivation converts nothing.** Only a registration `_ensure_registration` reports as
  `registration.added` converts, so a partner is not credited twice for one course.
- **An application converts when it is started, not submitted.** Starting is where the learner acted
  on the referral; a form may be finished weeks later under unrelated touches.
- **An anonymous application converts when it is attached to an account,** with `occurred_at` still
  the application's `created_at`, after the request's cookie is flushed to that account.
- **A claim that creates an account converts twice.** The `account_signup` conversion resolves at the
  claim and the `course_application` conversion at the application's `created_at`, so they may freeze
  different codes. Both are true.
- **Best-effort means a lost write is lost.** Every touch and conversion write is caught and reported;
  nothing retries it. A conversion recorded after a claim's transaction commits is lost if the request
  dies in between.
- **`referral_tracking` gains no dependency.** The three product apps call into it, and a `Conversion`
  points at its target through a `GenericForeignKey`, as `ObjectRoleAssignment` does
  (`freedom_ls/role_based_permissions/models.py`).
- **A conversion stores code text, not a foreign key to `ReferralCode`.** A hand-typed `?ref=` that
  matches no row really happened, and codes are never reused, so the text is stable. A payout query
  matches text to a row with `lookup_referral_code(site, code)`.
- **A later touch tallies nothing.** `FirstTouchCount` is a conversion-rate denominator;
  `increment_first_touch` stays on the minting path only.
- **A touch whose seven attribution key values are all blank is never recorded,** in the cookie or
  live. A click-id-only landing (`?gclid=` alone) can never put a code in play.
- **A repeat of the latest touch is skipped, in the cookie and live.** Seven identical key values to
  the most recent touch add nothing. A, B, A is still three touches. Two overlapping signed-in requests
  can both write a live repeat; a duplicate changes neither code, so that race is accepted.

## Requirements

### 1. `AttributionTouch` (`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/models.py`)

A `SiteAwareModel`, append-only. No IP, user agent, referer, raw query or click identifier: those stay
on `SignupAttribution`.

| Field | Type | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `user` | `ForeignKey(AUTH_USER_MODEL, on_delete=CASCADE, related_name="attribution_touches")` | |
| `advert_code`, `referral_code`, `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, `utm_term` | `CharField(blank=True, max_length=CAPS[...])` | The seven `ATTRIBUTION_KEY_FIELDS`, existing caps. |
| `landing_path` | `CharField(blank=True, max_length=CAPS["landing_path"])` | Set on a live touch and a flushed first touch; blank on a flushed list entry. |
| `occurred_at` | `DateTimeField(db_index=True)` | When the landing happened. Not `auto_now_add`: a flushed touch happened before the flush. |
| `recorded_at` | `DateTimeField(auto_now_add=True)` | When the row was written. |
| `recorded_via` | `CharField(max_length=16, choices=TouchSource)` | `live`, `signup_flush` or `login_flush`. Tells a reader how far to trust the ordering. |
| `key_hash` | `CharField(max_length=64)` | `counters.attribution_key_hash` over the seven key values. Makes the row idempotent. |

`Meta`:

- `UniqueConstraint(fields=["site", "user", "occurred_at", "key_hash"], name="unique_attribution_touch")`,
  so a flush that runs twice writes each touch once.
- `Index(fields=["site", "user", "occurred_at"])` for the resolver.
- `Index(fields=["site", "referral_code"])` for the payout query.
- `Index(fields=["site", "recorded_at"])` for the `apis-for-lead-monitoring-6-conversions` cursor.
- `ordering = ["-occurred_at"]`.

`save()` carries the same append-only guard as `SignupAttribution.save()`, in the same shape and with
the same comment. `TouchSource` is a `models.TextChoices` beside `Door`. The model stays out of
`TIMESTAMPED_MODELS` (`freedom_ls/contrib/conformance/tests/test_timestamped_models.py`), like every
model in this app.

### 2. `Conversion` (`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/models.py`)

A `SiteAwareModel`, append-only by the same guard.

| Field | Type | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `user` | `ForeignKey(AUTH_USER_MODEL, on_delete=CASCADE, related_name="referral_conversions")` | |
| `kind` | `CharField(max_length=32, choices=ConversionKind)` | `account_signup`, `course_application`, `course_interest`, `course_registration`. |
| `content_type` | `ForeignKey(ContentType, on_delete=CASCADE, null=True, blank=True)` | |
| `object_id` | `CharField(max_length=255, blank=True)` | `CharField`, as `ObjectRoleAssignment.object_id`: this app cannot import the target models and must not assume their pk type. |
| `target` | `GenericForeignKey("content_type", "object_id")` | `User`, `CourseApplication`, `CourseInterest` or `LearnerCourseRegistration`. Resolve it one row at a time, never with `prefetch_related`: `object_id` is text and the targets' pks are not. |
| `target_label` | `CharField(max_length=255, blank=True)` | Frozen at write time: the course title, or `"Account signup"`. The admin and export read this and never resolve `target`; it survives the target being deleted. |
| `occurred_at` | `DateTimeField(db_index=True)` | Supplied by the caller. |
| `recorded_at` | `DateTimeField(auto_now_add=True)` | |
| `window_days` | `PositiveSmallIntegerField()` | `REFERRAL_TRACKING_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_DAYS` as it was when the pair was resolved. |
| `first_touch` | `ForeignKey(AttributionTouch, on_delete=RESTRICT, null=True, blank=True, related_name="conversions_as_first")` | |
| `last_touch` | `ForeignKey(AttributionTouch, on_delete=RESTRICT, null=True, blank=True, related_name="conversions_as_last")` | |
| `first_referral_code` | `CharField(blank=True, max_length=CAPS["referral_code"])` | Frozen copy. |
| `last_referral_code` | `CharField(blank=True, max_length=CAPS["referral_code"])` | Frozen copy. |

`RESTRICT`, not `PROTECT`, on both touch keys. A touch a conversion rests on cannot be deleted on its
own, so pruning cannot remove it. Deleting the user still works, because the conversion goes in the
same cascade; `PROTECT` would raise there and block account erasure.

`Meta`:

- `UniqueConstraint(fields=["site", "kind", "content_type", "object_id"], name="unique_conversion_per_target")`,
  so a caller that fires twice records once.
- `Index(fields=["content_type", "object_id"])`.
- `Index(fields=["site", "kind", "occurred_at"])`.
- `Index(fields=["site", "recorded_at"])` for the API cursor.
- `ordering = ["-occurred_at"]`.

Both models are added to `USER_ERASURE_CASCADE_MODELS` in `referral_tracking/admin.py`, beside
`SignupAttribution`, so the account-erasure confirmation names them.

### 3. The cookie (`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/capture.py`)

**First-touch keys do not change.** `COOKIE_KEYS` and the `dict[str, str]` that
`read_attribution_cookie` returns stay exactly as they are. First-Class-LMS builds model fields, admin
fieldsets and `LeadSubmission(**cookie)` from both, so adding a key to either would break it on a
visitor's second landing.

**A separate touch-list key.** A new constant `COOKIE_TOUCHES_KEY = "tl"`, outside `COOKIE_KEYS` (`t`
is taken by `utm_term`). Its value is a list of entries, each holding the seven `ATTRIBUTION_KEY_FIELDS`
under their existing short keys (blank values omitted) plus `ts`, an ISO 8601 time. No landing path,
referer, raw query or click identifier.

**Validation.** `_is_first_touch_payload` keeps its rule for every key except `tl`, which must be a
list of dicts whose keys are the seven attribution short keys or `ts`, whose values are all `str`, and
whose `ts` parses with `datetime.fromisoformat`. Anything else fails the whole payload, which is then
treated as absent, as today.

**Reading.** A new `read_cookie_touches(request) -> list[CookieTouch]` returns the list (a `TypedDict`
or dataclass, no `Any`), empty for a cookie minted before this ships. It shares one decode with
`read_attribution_cookie`. Both reject a cookie whose `first_seen` is more than
`REFERRAL_TRACKING_COOKIE_MAX_AGE_DAYS` old: `get_signed_cookie(max_age=...)` checks the signature's
age, and every re-set signs afresh.

**Appending.** `append_touch(touches, first_touch, request)` builds an entry from the request and
appends it, unless all seven values are blank, or they equal the last entry's (or, with an empty list,
the frozen first touch's). The list holds at most `REFERRAL_TRACKING_MAX_COOKIE_TOUCHES` entries; at
the cap the oldest is evicted, so the frozen first touch and the newest entry always survive.

**Setting.** `set_attribution_cookie(response, first_touch, *, touches=())` keeps working with one
argument for the mint call. It sheds `COOKIE_DROP_ORDER` fields first, unchanged. If the payload still
exceeds `COOKIE_MAX_ENCODED_LENGTH`, list entries go oldest first, never the newest. If the first touch
plus the newest entry still does not fit, it returns `False` and sets nothing. On the append path that
means the new landing is not recorded and the browser keeps its previous cookie.

**Expiry that does not roll.** `max_age` is `_cookie_max_age()` minus the time since the frozen
`first_seen`, floored at zero; the mint call gets the full `_cookie_max_age()`. A re-set never pushes
expiry past 90 days after the first touch, so a cookie-carried touch can only reach a row within that
time. A live touch has no such limit.

**Clearing.** `request_cookie_clear(request)` and `is_cookie_clear_requested(request)`, a request-flag
pair shaped like `suppress_capture` / `is_capture_suppressed`. The flush sets it; the middleware acts
on it.

### 4. The middleware (`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/middleware.py`)

`AttributionCaptureMiddleware` still reads the request before the view runs and writes nothing until
the view returns. It gains three behaviours.

1. **Honour the clear flag on any request.** The early return for a non-GET or untracked request runs
   `get_response`, then checks `is_cookie_clear_requested`; when set, `response.delete_cookie` with the
   name and path the cookie was set under. The signup and login POSTs are neither GETs nor tracked.
2. **A signed-in tracked GET writes a row, not a cookie.** The touch and its time are taken from the
   request **before** `get_response`, so a conversion the view itself starts sees the touch in play.
   After the view returns, when `request.user.is_authenticated` and the site resolves to a `Site`, it
   calls `record_live_touch` with that time and leaves the cookie alone: it mints no cookie, appends
   nothing and tallies nothing, with or without a cookie present. The mint-and-tally branch is
   anonymous-only, so a later login flush can never record the same landing a second time. `request.user` is read only
   here, so an untracked request still resolves no session. The extra cost is the session and user
   reads (none if the view already made them), the latest-touch read, and the insert in its savepoint;
   a test pins the count.
3. **An anonymous tracked GET with a cookie appends to it.** It reads the payload, calls
   `append_touch` and re-sets the cookie with `touches=`. It tallies nothing and issues no query. The
   first-touch branch, with no cookie present, is unchanged: mint, then tally.

`Vary: Cookie` and `private, no-store` apply to the append response as to the mint response.

### 5. Writing touches and conversions (`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/conversions.py`, new)

Every entry point is best-effort: `transaction.atomic()`, catch `DatabaseError`, report with
`sentry_sdk.capture_exception`, return `None` (or an empty list). `record_hit` in `hits.py` and
`record_attribution_on_signup` already do this; extract one shared helper rather than writing a third
copy.

**`record_live_touch(*, request, user, site, occurred_at) -> AttributionTouch | None`** writes one
touch with `recorded_via=live`, field values from `first_touch_from_request` and `key_hash` from
`attribution_key_hash`. It writes nothing when all seven values are blank or the user's most recent
touch on that site has the same `key_hash`.

**`flush_cookie_touches(*, request, user, source) -> list[AttributionTouch]`** writes one touch per
cookie touch: the frozen first touch at its `first_seen` (with its `landing_path`), then each list entry
at its `ts`, all with `recorded_via=source`. It writes through `AttributionTouch._base_manager` with
`site=user.site`, because the `user_logged_in` signal can fire outside a request's site context, and
does nothing when `request` is `None`. `get_or_create` on the unique key, so a touch already flushed is
not written again. An entry whose `ts` is later than now or earlier than `first_seen` is skipped. It
then calls `request_cookie_clear(request)`. Touches left by an earlier visitor on a shared device go to
the first account to sign up or log in there; that is accepted.

**`record_conversion(*, user, kind, target, occurred_at, target_label) -> Conversion | None`** resolves
the pair and writes the row with `get_or_create` on the unique key, copying each touch's
`referral_code` into the frozen column and the current window into `window_days`.

Resolution lives in **`freedom_ls/referral_tracking/resolution.py`**:
`touches_in_play(*, user, site, when) -> tuple[AttributionTouch | None, AttributionTouch | None]`. It
reads that user's touches on that site with a non-blank `referral_code` and
`when - window <= occurred_at <= when`, and returns the earliest and latest, or `(None, None)`.
`site` is explicit so a shell script or background job can call it without a request.

### 6. The call sites

Each fires once, on creation only, with no error handling of its own.

- **Signup.** `record_attribution_on_signup` (`signals.py`) writes `SignupAttribution` first, unchanged,
  then `flush_cookie_touches(source=signup_flush)`, then `record_conversion` with
  `kind=account_signup`, `target=user`, `occurred_at=attribution.signed_up_at`,
  `target_label="Account signup"`. The flush comes first so its touches exist. It also runs for an
  account a claim creates, since `attach_claimed_application` sends `user_signed_up`.
- **Login.** A new `user_logged_in` receiver in `signals.py`, connected by the existing `ready()`,
  calls `flush_cookie_touches(source=login_flush)` and nothing else.
- **Signed-in application.** `_start_application` (`course_applications/views.py`) surfaces the
  `created` flag from its `get_or_create`. When `created` and a user was passed: `kind=course_application`,
  `target=app`, `occurred_at=app.created_at`, `target_label=course.title`. An anonymous start converts
  nothing here.
- **Anonymous application, once attached.** `attach_claimed_application` (`course_applications/claims.py`,
  after its signal) and `adopt_anonymous_applications_on_login` (`course_applications/receivers.py`),
  when the attach kept the incoming application (`result.application.pk == application.pk`), call
  `flush_cookie_touches` for that user and then `record_conversion` with `kind=course_application`,
  `target` the attached application, `occurred_at` its `created_at`, and `target_label` the course
  title. The flush source is `signup_flush` when the claim created the account, `login_flush`
  otherwise. It runs here as well as in the signup and login receivers because receiver order is not
  guaranteed; running it twice writes nothing twice. When the account's submitted application wins,
  nothing converts.
- **Interest.** `_express_interest` (`course_interest/views.py`), on `created`, beside
  `record_interest_expressed`: `kind=course_interest`, `target=interest`,
  `occurred_at=interest.created_at`, `target_label=course.title`.
- **Self-registration.** `self_register` (`learner_management/learner_actions.py`) returns the new
  `LearnerCourseRegistration`, or `None` when nothing was added, instead of a `bool`; its tests change
  to match. `initiate_course_access` (`learner_interface/views.py`), on a returned registration, beside
  `record_course_self_registered`: `kind=course_registration`, `target=registration`,
  `occurred_at=registration.registered_at`, `target_label=course.title`.

### 7. Settings and checks (`config.py`, `checks.py`)

| Setting | Default | Governs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `REFERRAL_TRACKING_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_DAYS` | `90` | How far back `touches_in_play` looks. Equal to the cookie's life, so a flushed touch is never already out of window. |
| `REFERRAL_TRACKING_MAX_COOKIE_TOUCHES` | `10` | Entries in the cookie's touch list before the oldest is evicted. |

Both declared in `declared_settings` with defaults, neither required.

`checks.py` gains `freedom_ls_referral_tracking.W001`: a warning when
`REFERRAL_TRACKING_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_DAYS` is less than `REFERRAL_TRACKING_COOKIE_MAX_AGE_DAYS`.

### 8. Admin and export (`admin.py`, `resources.py`)

`AttributionTouchAdmin` and `ConversionAdmin` mirror `SignupAttributionAdmin`:
`SiteAwareExportModelAdmin`, fully read-only, `date_hierarchy = "occurred_at"`,
`list_select_related = ["user"]`, searchable by `user__email`. Each has a resource subclassing
`SiteAwareModelResource` that omits `site` and exports `user` by email, as
`SignupAttributionResource` does. The shared base resource is not changed.

- `AttributionTouchAdmin` lists the codes, UTM source and campaign, `occurred_at` and `recorded_via`,
  and filters on `recorded_via`.
- `ConversionAdmin` lists user, `kind`, `target_label`, both codes and `occurred_at`, and filters on
  `kind` and on **code in play**: a subclass of `unfold.contrib.filters.admin.TextFilter` matching
  conversions whose `first_referral_code` or `last_referral_code` equals the entered code,
  case-insensitive. No column resolves `target`.
- `ConversionResource` exports `id`, user email, `kind`, `target_label`, `occurred_at`,
  `first_referral_code`, `first_touch.occurred_at`, `last_referral_code`, `last_touch.occurred_at` and
  `window_days`, with both touches loaded by `select_related`.

The export must export the filtered queryset, not the whole table.

### 9. `prune_attribution_touches` (management command)

A `djclick` command shaped on `prune_referral_code_hits`: `--older-than-days N` with
`click.IntRange(min=1)`, no default, across every site, in primary-key batches of `PRUNE_BATCH_SIZE`,
printing the total. It deletes only touches no conversion references
(`conversions_as_first__isnull=True, conversions_as_last__isnull=True`). The docstring says `RESTRICT`
is the guarantee and the filter keeps the command from tripping over it.

## Testing

Use `mock_site_context` for anything touching a `SiteAwareModel` and `django_assert_num_queries` for
query-count pins. Build touches through factories except where the middleware is the subject.

**Tests that change** (`referral_tracking/tests/test_middleware.py` unless named):

- `test_second_tracked_get_in_the_same_client_leaves_the_cookie_byte_identical`: a second tracked GET
  with a different key appends one entry; one with the same key leaves the cookie byte-identical.
- `test_a_tracked_get_with_an_existing_valid_cookie_costs_no_query`: keeps zero queries for an
  anonymous visitor, and gains a signed-in sibling pinning the live-touch count.
- `test_a_visitor_holding_the_cookie_is_logged_as_a_hit_and_keeps_the_first_touch`: also expects an
  appended entry.
- `test_a_tracked_get_with_an_existing_valid_cookie_sets_no_cookie`: holds only for a repeat of the
  same key.
- `test_a_tracked_get_with_an_existing_valid_cookie_carries_vary_cookie`: also asserts
  `private, no-store` on an append response.
- `test_signup_and_confirmation_together_leave_exactly_one_row`: checked; still one
  `SignupAttribution`.
- `test_a_multibyte_worst_case_cookie_fits_the_browser_limit` (`test_capture.py`): the encoded cookie
  never exceeds the limit with any number of worst-case entries.
- `self_register`'s tests, for its new return value.

`..._leaves_the_tally_at_one` is unchanged and proves a repeat landing tallies nothing.

**Cases a requirement does not already spell out:**

- A cookie with a list still makes `read_attribution_cookie` return exactly the `COOKIE_KEYS` names.
- A worst-case landing that cannot fit beside the first touch leaves the previous cookie in place.
- A re-signed cookie whose `first_seen` is over 90 days old reads as absent; one at exactly 90 days is
  re-set with `max_age` zero.
- A signed-in GET of a tracked URL whose view starts an application puts that touch's code in play
  for the application's conversion.
- A `?gclid=`-only landing appends nothing and writes nothing.
- A signup after three tracked landings writes three `signup_flush` touches at the landing times, and
  the signup response deletes the cookie. Signup then login writes each touch once. A login with no
  cookie, or with `request=None`, writes nothing and raises nothing.
- Touches at T-60, T-30 and T-1 days carrying A, B and C: a conversion at T freezes A and C. A touch
  after T, one older than the window (for both codes), a UTM-only touch between coded ones, and another
  site's or user's touch all change nothing.
- Each call site writes one row with the right `kind`, `target`, `occurred_at` and `target_label`,
  and one row when called twice. A reactivating `initiate_course_access` writes none. An anonymous
  application converts on claim and on adoption at login, with the claiming request's cookie touches in
  play; an erased incoming application converts nothing.
- A `DatabaseError` writing a conversion leaves the application, interest or registration committed and
  the request successful, per call site.
- Saving an existing touch or conversion raises; the admin change view persists nothing.
- Deleting a user whose touches are referenced by their conversions succeeds through the real
  collector and removes both. Deleting a referenced touch directly raises `RestrictedError`; the prune
  skips it.
- The code-in-play filter matches first or last, case-insensitively, and an export after filtering
  holds only matching rows, with the listed columns. A `ConversionAdmin` changelist page resolves no
  generic FK.
- W001 fires when the window is shorter than the cookie's life.

## Open questions

- **A consent gate is overdue.** FLS sets the tracking cookie on any tracked landing whatever the
  visitor agreed to. The ICO's April 2026 storage and access guidance puts affiliate attribution on the
  consent-required side. An operator in the UK or EU needs an answer before this goes live, and FLS
  needs a spec for it. The `cookie-banner` idea is a notice, not a gate.
- **Who sets the credit rule.** FLS records first and last and picks neither. The programme owner has
  to write first-click or last-click into the partner terms before the first payout.
- **Self-referral and typed codes.** Nothing stops a partner using their own code on their own account,
  or a signed-in learner typing `?ref=` to add a touch. This work makes both worth doing for the first
  time. Detection is out of scope.
- **Whether a partner should ever see any of this.** Everything here is admin-only.

## Success criteria

- A person who lands through code A, returns three weeks later through code B, signs up and applies
  for a course has two touches and two conversions, the application's freezing A as first and B as
  last.
- An existing account that follows a partner's link, logs in and expresses interest produces a
  conversion naming that code.
- An anonymous applicant who arrived through code A and claims the application has a
  `course_application` conversion freezing A, dated when they applied.
- A `DatabaseError` in attribution leaves the application, interest and registration committed and the
  request successful.
